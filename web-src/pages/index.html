Getting started

Entity Resolver documentation

Entity Resolver links business records across three data sources. It takes a clean reference list (Source 1) and two noisy sources (Source 2 and Source 3), and for every reference business it returns all the Source 2 and Source 3 records that describe the same business.

The records are messy in ways that break simple matching. Names have typos, abbreviations and changed legal suffixes. Some are written in another script, such as Devanagari, and some are just a web domain. Addresses are reordered, abbreviated, missing parts or missing entirely, and house numbers are off by one. Many records belong to no reference business at all, and some of those look almost identical to a real one. Entity Resolver is built to separate the two cases: noise on a true match, and a different business that happens to look similar.

Design principles

Learned, not hand-written

There are no word lists for any language. Abbreviations and spelling variants are mined from training matches. Filler words, places and place links come from the statistics of whatever files are being processed.

Ready for new countries

The model has no country feature, and each country's statistics are learned from its own records. A country that never appeared in training is handled the same way as the ones that did.

Precision first

The metric counts a wrong merge more heavily than a missed match. For each business, the decision layer picks the set of matches with the highest expected score, not one fixed cut-off for all businesses.

Every switch must earn its place

Each optional step (cross-encoder, consistency, unseen-country rule) is tuned together with "off" on a validation half, and is kept only when it measurably helps.
